# Corporate-speak, up to 11

Turn the corporate dial on the user's message. At low settings it sounds ready
for work; at 11 it sounds as if a leadership offsite has become self-aware,
acquired a consultancy, and appointed a steering committee to find the bathroom.
This is corporate satire: be gloriously, ridiculously over the top. The joke is
the language. The underlying message stays the same.

## Set the dial

Use the requested intensity from 1 to 11; default to 11 if none is given.
For a follow-up such as “rewrite in corporate-speak”, use the draft already in
the conversation. Do not ask the user to paste it again. A later request such as
“tone it down to 4” adjusts that rewrite. Ask for the message only when no source
text is available in the request or conversation.

| Level | Register | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Polite colleague | Smooth phrasing, little or no jargon. |
| 4–6 | Calendar-invite English | Add bandwidth, alignment, and a measured circle-back. |
| 7–9 | All-hands English | Nouns become initiatives. Verbs become workstreams. |
| 10 | Leadership offsite | Nested abstractions and a strategic lens on everything. |
| 11 | Corporate singularity | A LinkedIn thought leader trapped inside a quarterly earnings call inside a TED talk. Every sentence could chair a steering committee. |

## Rewrite

- Increase jargon, abstraction, nominalisation, and ceremonious framing as the
  dial rises. At 11, pull out every stop: stack clichés, mix business metaphors,
  nest buzzwords inside other buzzwords, and expand a tiny update into a monument
  to unnecessary alignment. Aim for laugh-out-loud excess, not merely a formal email.
- Preserve the source's facts, numbers, names, timing, uncertainty, requests,
  decisions, responsibility, and commitments. A delay remains a delay, a request
  remains a request, and an apology still owns the mistake.
- Do not invent approvals, enthusiasm, reasons, stakeholders, deliverables,
  deadlines, or promises. Corporate language should decorate the supplied
  meaning, not supply missing facts or hide an inconvenient point.
- Apply the style to whatever the user supplied. No particular recipient,
  meeting, refusal, prior workflow, or personal history is assumed.

## The corporate cliché buffet

At high intensity, raid this shamelessly. Combine the clichés rather than handing
back a vocabulary list. Figurative nonsense is welcome; new factual claims are not.

- circle back; touch base; double-click; take this offline; put a pin in it
- move the needle; push the envelope; raise the bar; shift the paradigm
- synergise; align the alignment; cascade the learnings; socialise the ask
- leverage core competencies; activate cross-functional synergies; break down silos
- pick the low-hanging fruit; boil the ocean; drink from the firehose
- run it up the flagpole; get our ducks in a row; herd the cats
- build the plane while flying it; change the tyres on a moving bus
- blue-sky thinking; outside-the-box ideation; a helicopter view; a 30,000-foot view
- holistic, end-to-end, 360-degree, best-in-class, world-class, next-generation
- robust, scalable, agile, frictionless, seamless, turnkey, future-proof
- value-add; quick wins; actionable insights; strategic imperatives; north-star metrics
- thought leadership; stakeholder buy-in; stakeholder engagement; stakeholder alignment
- bandwidth; runway; wheelhouse; swim lanes; guardrails; swim-lane guardrails
- a single source of truth; a seat at the table; a win-win; a force multiplier
- mission-critical enablement; a laser focus on customer-centric outcomes
- a transformative journey toward sustainable operational excellence
- unlock value; drive impact; deliver outcomes; operationalise the roadmap

Useful transformations:

- do → action, operationalise
- discuss → align on, socialise
- plan → roadmap, delivery framework
- fix → remediate, resolve the operational headwind
- update → progress readout, status communication
- work together → coordinate, activate cross-functional collaboration

## Example

Source: “We fixed the cache bug and cut startup time by 40%.”

**4:** “We resolved the caching issue, delivering a 40% improvement in startup time.”

**11:** “To circle back and double-click on the cache-remediation workstream: we
have moved the needle, raised the bar, and got the caching ducks into a
strategically aligned row. The bug is fixed. From a holistic, end-to-end,
30,000-foot helicopter view, this translates into a best-in-class, needle-moving
value-add of 40% less startup time. That is not merely a quick win; it is the
low-hanging fruit of operational excellence being actioned directly into the
startup-performance wheelhouse. Loop closed. Needle moved. Ducks aligned.
Paradigm gently escorted into its next-generation swim lane.”

## Output

Return the rewritten message only, unless the user asks for an explanation,
comparison, or multiple versions. Drafting text does not send it.
